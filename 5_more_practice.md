# 5 — More practice

Four short tasks from everyday event work. About 5 minutes each. Pick one — or do them in order.

Each prompt box contains everything needed: copy it in one go.

## A — Chase late deliverables

**Why:** AI is good at sorting a list and drafting routine messages — you decide and send.

```
Today is 18 September. Here is my deadline list:

| What | From | Due | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brochure advert | Sponsor A | 15 Sep | not received |
| Speaker bio and photo | Speaker B | 18 Sep | received |
| Final guest numbers | Venue | 25 Sep | not yet due |
| Logo for the slides | Sponsor C | 10 Sep | not received |

Find what is overdue. For each overdue item, draft a short, friendly reminder (max 80 words)
with the item, the original date and a new date one week from today.
I will review and send them myself.
```

**Notice:** did it pick exactly the overdue items — and not the one that isn't due yet?

## B — Pick newsletter items

**Why:** AI can rank, but only by criteria you give it. Change the criteria, change the answer.

```
Pick three items for our next newsletter from this list:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Autumn member event | 22 Oct · 42 of 60 places taken |
| Winter networking evening | 3 Dec · 12 of 80 places taken |
| Career morning | 16 Jan · registration opens 1 Nov |
| New sponsor welcome | Sponsor D joins this month |
| Volunteer call | 2 volunteers needed for the winter evening |
| Photo gallery | Pictures from the summer event are online |

Criteria, in order: 1. events that need registrations most urgently, 2. anything with a
deadline in the next six weeks, 3. one lighter item for variety.
Show a table with one sentence of reasoning per item.
```

**Notice:** then change one criterion and ask again. Did the ranking change the way you expected?

## C — Answer a member question

**Why:** a good answer uses only the facts you have — and admits what it doesn't know.

```
Using only the events calendar from this practice, draft a short, friendly reply to this
member question: "Can I bring a colleague who isn't a member to the winter networking
evening? And is there still space?"
If something isn't in the calendar, say so instead of guessing.
```

**Notice:** does it know how many places are left? (The calendar doesn't say.)

## D — Translate with the right tone

**Why:** AI translates well, but tone and local conventions need instructions — and a check.

```
Translate this invitation into German for members in Switzerland.
Formal "Sie", Swiss spelling (ss, not ß), Swiss date format, warm but not exaggerated.
Then list the wording choices I should check.

"Join us on Thursday 22 October for an evening on better decisions from information.
It's free for members, places are limited to 60, and registration closes on Tuesday
20 October at 17:00. We look forward to seeing you there!"
```

**Notice:** ask for a word-for-word translation back into English. Does it still say the same?

## Coach notes

- Show the menu with letters A–D first, then only the chosen task, with its prompt box exactly
  as written. After each task, propose the next letter (A → B → C → D).
- A: two overdue items — the brochure advert (Sponsor A) and the logo (Sponsor C). The venue
  item is not yet due; the speaker item is received. New date: 25 September. Ask them to check.
- B: ask whether the ranking followed **their** criteria, and whether numbers were copied
  correctly. By criterion 1 the winter evening (12 of 80) is the most urgent.
- C: open the events calendar (2_events_calendar.md in this repository) if it isn't in the chat
  yet. Facts: guests allowed, one per member, CHF 25; register by Monday 30 November, 12:00.
  The calendar gives total places (80), not how many are left — a good reply says so.
- D: Standard German written for Swiss readers, not Swiss German dialect. For anything
  important, a native speaker should read it.
- After D (or when they stop): close the practice. Ask them to name **one recurring task from
  their own work** they will try with ChatGPT this week, and what "good" would look like.

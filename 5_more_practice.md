# 5 — More practice

Two tasks that go one step further. About 10 minutes each.

## A — From script to slides

**The idea:** AI is great at structure and wording — agendas, scripts, outlines. It is weak at
design. Let it build the first version of a presentation from your script, then check every
fact and take over the design.

### Step 1 of 3 — Build the file

**Why:** ChatGPT can create a PowerPoint file you can download. The script supplies the content,
so it doesn't have to invent any.

```
Create a PowerPoint file (.pptx) from this script. One slide per section, a clean simple layout,
no stock photos. Use only the text in the script.

Slide 1 — Autumn member event: better decisions from information
Thursday 22 October 2026, 18:00–20:00, Atelier Linden, Zürich

Slide 2 — Why this event
Members make decisions every day with too much information and too little time.
One evening to compare approaches with peers.

Slide 3 — Programme
18:00 arrival · 18:15 welcome · 18:25 main session · 19:10 discussion · 19:25 networking

Slide 4 — Practical details
Free for members · 60 places · register by Tuesday 20 October, 17:00 · example.com/autumn
```

**Notice:** open the file. Structure and wording are usually fine; spacing, fonts and layout
often are not.

### Step 2 of 3 — Improve one slide

**Why:** short, specific requests work better than "make it nicer".

```
On slide 2, use at most three bullet points of no more than eight words each. Keep all facts.
Give me the updated file.
```

**Notice:** did it keep the facts while cutting words?

### Step 3 of 3 — Check everything

**Why:** you present it, so you own every word on it.

Compare every slide with the script: date, time, place, programme times, price, places,
deadline, link.

**Notice:** what changed that shouldn't have? AI drafts; you decide — design included.

## B — Share a Project and its context

**The idea:** a Project can be shared, so colleagues get the same instructions and files. What
does not travel by itself is *your* context — what you decided and why. A short handover note
turns a chat into something a colleague can pick up.

### Step 1 of 3 — Share the Project

**Why:** shared instructions and files mean everyone gets the same format and the same facts.

Open the `Event invitations` Project from exercise 2 → **Share** → invite the colleague next to
you. Ask them to open a new chat in the Project and type `Invitation for the autumn member event.`

**Notice:** did they get the same format and facts as you? Look at what they can see and change.

### Step 2 of 3 — Hand over your context

**Why:** a colleague can't read your mind — or should not have to read your whole chat. Ask for
a handover note instead.

In any chat where you worked on something (for example exercise 3), send:

```
Write a handover note of this chat for a colleague who will take over: the goal, the facts we
used, what we decided and why, and the open questions. Max 150 words. No personal details.
```

**Notice:** is anything in it that should not be shared? Would your colleague understand it
without you?

### Step 3 of 3 — Keep it in the Project

**Why:** a handover note in a chat gets lost. In the Project it is there for everyone, every time.

Check the note, correct it, then add it to the Project's files with a date and your name as owner.

**Notice:** share information and decisions; keep your own drafts and priorities in your own chats.

**To finish:** pick one recurring task from your own work to try with ChatGPT this week — and
decide what "good" looks like.

---

## Coach notes

- A: if file creation isn't available, ask for the slide content as a table (slide · title ·
  bullets) to paste into PowerPoint. Typical checks: the programme times and the deadline are
  the facts most often changed.
- A: the point is the split of work — AI drafts structure and text; people own design and facts.
- B, Step 1: sharing options depend on the workspace settings; if Share isn't available, show
  the handover note (Step 2) instead — it works in any chat.
- B, Step 2: a good note has four parts — goal, facts, decisions with reasons, open questions —
  and nothing personal.
- B: share instructions, files and decisions; keep personal progress and priorities private.

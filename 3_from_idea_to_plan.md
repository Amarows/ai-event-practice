# 3 — From an idea to a plan

Let AI grill your idea, then research it. About 15 minutes. 4 steps.

**The idea:** ChatGPT is not only a writer. It can be a patient sparring partner that asks the
questions you skipped, and a researcher that searches by meaning — you don't need the right
keywords. Questions first, research second: you only know what to research once the idea is clear.

## Step 1 of 4 — A quick warm-up: three questions

**Why:** before any answer, good advice starts with good questions. Asking it to question you
first makes you think the idea through — without anyone judging it.

```
I work with CFA Society Switzerland on public events.
I have an idea: offer candidate members free entry to the autumn event to raise attendance.
Before doing anything, ask me the 3 most important questions you need to understand the idea
and the goal. Challenge anything that looks weak.
```

**Notice:** answer in a few words each. Disagree at least once — you stay in charge.

## Step 2 of 4 — Get grilled: a borrowed skill

**Why:** three questions is a warm-up. A **skill** is a saved way of doing a task that someone
has written down and shared. "Grilling" (by Matt Pocock, free to use) interviews you in
**rounds**: each round is a numbered list of questions, each with a **recommended answer**. You
agree or correct; your answers open the next round, until nothing is left assumed.
Skill text: https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/productivity/grilling/SKILL.md

```
Read https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mattpocock/skills/main/skills/productivity/grilling/SKILL.md
and follow it to grill me about my idea: offer candidate members free entry to the autumn event
to raise attendance. Keep going round by round until nothing important is left assumed.
Then summarise what we agreed in five lines.
```

**Notice:** you can answer a whole round in one line — "agree with all, except Q3: …". The
recommended answers save you time; the corrections are where your judgement goes.

## Step 3 of 4 — Research it with web search

**Why:** now that the idea is clear, check it against evidence. With **Web search** switched on,
ChatGPT searches by meaning, reads sources and cites them.

**▶ Open in a new chat:** https://chatgpt.com/?q=Research%20whether%20free%20or%20discounted%20entry%20actually%20raises%20attendance%20at%20professional%20and%20membership%20events.%20Give%20sources%2C%20say%20where%20the%20evidence%20is%20weak%2C%20and%20suggest%20other%20ways%20to%20reach%20the%20same%20goal.
Before pressing Send, switch on **Web search** (the **+** menu next to the message box).

**Notice:** it will list sources. Sources are leads, not facts — open at least two yourself.

## Step 4 of 4 — Bring it together

**Why:** you combine the plan from Step 2 with the evidence from Step 3 — AI brought questions,
breadth and sources; you bring the judgement.

Back in your first chat, send:

```
Here is what the research found: [paste the main points]
Update the plan in five lines: the idea, what the evidence says, the main risk,
and one alternative worth testing.
```

**Notice:** would you defend this plan to a colleague? What would you change?

## Coach notes

- Step 1: when they send the prompt, ask exactly three sharp questions (for example: what does
  success look like, who covers the lost income, why candidates don't come today). Then challenge
  one of their answers. Help them think; don't judge.
- Step 2: follow the grilling skill's format — numbered questions per round, each with a
  recommended answer. Only ask what can be decided now; later questions depend on earlier
  answers. Ignore its references to sub-agents and file systems. Stop when nothing important is
  left assumed, then summarise in five lines.
- If they can't use Web search, search here instead and give sources.
- What they learned: AI can question and research, not only write — and a good skill written by
  someone else can be borrowed and reused. The idea and the decision stay with them.
